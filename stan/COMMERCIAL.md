# STAN — the funnel, the offer, and the money

**Marketing, communications and revenue in one place.** What STAN offers at each
stage of a person's journey, what can be billed, how it packages, when it can go
on sale, and how people find out it exists.

Every figure in §6 is an **assumption to test**, not a projection. They are
there so the shape of the business is visible, and so the first real numbers have
something to be compared against.

---

## 1. One principle before the funnel

**This audience anchors prices against gear, not against therapy.**

A cycle costs £150–400. A private bloods panel costs £80–150. A private TRT
clinic costs £100–300 a month. Against that, a £45 hour with someone who has
been there is *cheap*, and pricing it at £15 would signal that it is worthless.
Do not underprice. This is the one audience in health where a low price actively
costs you credibility.

### Stage 0 — The trigger

Not a stage STAN controls, but the whole design depends on understanding it. A
man does not wake up wanting harm reduction. Something happens: a panel comes
back he cannot read, he stops and the floor falls out, his partner finds the
vials, a mate collapses at the gym. **He is searching at 11pm with his heart
going.** Everything downstream is designed for that person in that state.

### Stage 1 — Awareness: he finds out STAN exists

| | |
|---|---|
| **What STAN offers** | The search bar that understands "knackered all the time". The six doors. Honest "not written yet" pages that still route onward. |
| **Value to him** | Somewhere that takes him seriously and does not lecture. |
| **Cost to him** | Nothing. No account. |
| **Revenue** | None. |
| **The ask** | *One thing only:* an email address, in exchange for being told when the thing he needed is written. This is where the list gets built, and the list is the asset that makes every later stage cheaper. |

Channels in §4.

### Stage 2 — First value: he gets something true, free

| | |
|---|---|
| **What STAN offers** | An answered question. A marker page. The consultation brief, downloadable. A recorded seminar. An open group he can sit in with his camera off. |
| **Value to him** | He learns something his GP did not tell him. He is less alone. |
| **Cost to him** | Nothing. |
| **Revenue** | None directly. **This is where trust is built, and trust is the entire asset.** A person who got something true for free is the only person who will pay later. |

The free tier is not a loss leader. It is the reason anyone believes the paid
tier. It stays free permanently, which the scope document already commits to.

### Stage 3 — First pound: small, low-risk, obviously worth it

This is where *traded time* begins.

| Product | What it is | Time traded | Price | Packagable when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bloods walkthrough** | 45 min, booked. He brings his panel. A trained peer walks through what each marker *is*, and helps him complete the consultation brief for his appointment. | Peer, 45 min | **£45** | **Now.** Founder delivers. Calendar link, payment link. |
| **Peer session** | 45 min with someone who has been where he is. Listening, orientation, what to ask. Not advice. | Peer, 45 min | **£45** | **Now.** Same mechanism. |
| **Live seminar** | 40 min from someone who knows, then questions. | Speaker, 60 min + prep | **Free live** — awareness. Recording goes to members. | When one speaker is booked. |

**The walkthrough is the wedge.** Deliverable this month, Tier A, needs no
clinician, ends with a referral. If men will not pay £45 for it, the rest of the
model needs rethinking — better to learn that in week one than month twelve.

**The line to hold, in the room:** the walkthrough teaches him to read a panel.
It does not read *his*. "This is what SHBG is and why it changes what your total
means" is education. "Your SHBG is high so your free T is low" is interpretation.
The first is a service; the second is a liability and a clinician's job.

### Stage 4 — Committed: a programme, not a session

| Product | What it is | Time traded | Price | Packagable when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Coming-off cohort** | Six weeks, same eight people, two facilitators. Starts together, finishes together. | 2 facilitators × 90 min × 6 | **£200–240** per person | Month 2–3. Needs a second facilitator and eight people ready. |
| **Staying-on programme** | For the man who is not stopping. Monthly check-in, monitoring plan, the markers to watch and why. | Peer, 45 min/month | **£35/month** | Month 3. Needs the monitoring content written. |
| **Bloods package** | Partner-lab panel plus the walkthrough plus a route to interpretation. | Peer, 45 min + lab | **£120–160** | When a lab partner exists. The scope document forbids selling a panel without a route to interpretation, so this waits for the panel. |

The cohort is the best unit economics in the whole model: eight people at £220
is £1,760 for nine hours of facilitation.

### Stage 5 — Clinical: the margin

| Product | What it is | Time traded | Price | Packagable when |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Clinical consultation via the STAN panel** | A vetted clinician who understands this population sees him. STAN books it and takes a published fee. | Clinician's, 30–45 min | Clinician sets price (typically £150–300). **STAN fee 15–25%, published on the page.** | When one panel clinician exists. Month 3–6. |
| **Clinical monitoring** | Ongoing, through the panel. | Clinician's | As above, recurring | As above. |

This is the stage that turns *signposting away* into *referral through*.
Signposting loses the user and earns nothing. Referral keeps the relationship
and funds the free tier. The fee is disclosed on the referral page — that is
what makes it compatible with the integrity the whole thing rests on.

### Stage 6 — Member: the moat

| Product | What it is | Price | Packagable when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monitoring membership** | Bloods tracked over time, quarterly walkthrough, priority booking, full recorded-seminar library, member-only groups. | **£15–25/month** | Month 6–9. Needs enough in the library to be worth it, and the bloods tracker built. |

Recurring revenue, and the reason he comes back every eight weeks with new bloods
for years. Men in this world already track everything in spreadsheets; give
them a proper place to do it and they do not leave.

### Stage 7 — Advocate

Not a product. He tells his gym. He shares the belief-register page. He becomes
a **paid peer supporter** — which is also the recruitment pipeline for Stage 3
delivery capacity. The best walkthrough facilitators will be men who had one.

## 4. Marketing — how they find out

Ranked by expected return for effort.

**1. Search — the question bank *is* the strategy.** Every one of the 68 questions,
and every one of the 186 phrasings, is a real query someone types. Every answered
question is a landing page for it. This is slow and it compounds, and it is the
primary channel for the whole life of the project. The claims architecture helps
here in a second way: increasingly people ask an AI, and AIs cite well-structured,
factual, attributed pages. Being the source that gets cited is the new front page.

**2. Needle and syringe programmes.** They see IPED users every day and have
nowhere to send them. A leaflet in every NSP in Scotland and England is the
highest-value physical distribution available, it reaches the people at highest
risk, and the staff will be grateful. The same records that render the website
render the leaflet.

**3. Gyms.** Posters with a QR code. A "we know about STAN" sticker on the door.
Gym owners often want to help and have no idea how; give them something that
costs nothing and makes them look responsible.

**4. Community presence — earned, never bought.** Reddit, forums, Discord. Not by
posting links, which gets you banned and deserves to. By being the account that
answers the question properly and links the STAN page when it is genuinely the
best answer. **The belief register is the shareable piece**: "twelve things
widely believed — three supported, two contradicted, seven never studied" is the
single most linkable thing STAN could publish, because it respects the reader.

**5. Clinician referral.** GPs have patients and nothing to offer them. Slow to
build, high value once built. The consultation brief is the way in — a GP who
receives a patient with a well-organised brief will ask where it came from.

**6. Press.** One good national piece drives more traffic than a year of SEO.
But not yet — see §5.

## 5. Communications and PR

**The story.** *"I used anabolic steroids for ten years. When I tried to stop,
nobody could help me — not my GP, not the NHS, not the forums. So I built the
thing that should have existed."* Lived experience, a documented NHS gap, real
clinical governance, and AI used honestly. That is a health-desk feature at a
national paper, and it only needs telling once, well.

**Timing.** Not before the wedge is live. A feature that sends ten thousand
people to a site with nothing to do wastes the one shot. PR follows product.

**Messaging discipline — three rules.**

1. **Never "steroids are bad."** Always "here is what is true." The moment STAN
   moralises it loses the audience it exists for and becomes every other site.
2. **Founder speaks to lived experience. Panel clinician speaks to clinical
   questions. Never the founder on clinical questions**, however well he knows
   the answer. This protects him, protects STAN, and is exactly the discipline the
   support model already sets.
3. **Lead with what STAN refuses to do.** No dosing protocols, no sourcing, no
   affiliate money, no guarantees. In this field the refusals are the credential.

**The trust artefacts for health journalists.** The literature list. The
corrections log, once it exists. The "we don't have this yet" honesty. These are
what separate STAN from every wellness startup a health journalist has been
burned by, and they are worth showing rather than describing.

**Spokespeople to develop.** The founder for the story. One panel clinician for
authority. One person who came through a cohort, eventually, for proof.

tier plus a grant. That is the structure working as designed.

### What would break the model

- **Men will not pay £45 for the walkthrough.** Test in month one. If true,
  everything above needs rethinking.
- **No clinician joins the panel.** Stage 5 and the margin never materialise.
  Everything else still works but the ceiling is much lower.
- **Membership retention under 6 months.** The moat leaks. Watch this number
  before anything else once membership exists.
- **The founder remains the only deliverer.** Capacity caps at ~10 sessions a
  week and burnout follows. Recruiting from the advocate stage is the fix.

## 7. What each of these needs from the website

| Stage | The website must |
|---|---|
| Awareness | Answer the search. Capture an email. Say clearly what is and is not ready. |
| First value | Deliver a true answer, a downloadable brief, a group time. |
| First £ | **Show the walkthrough as a bookable thing with a price.** Currently nothing on the site can be bought. |
| Committed | Show cohorts with dates, places left, and a waiting list. |
| Clinical | Show the panel — who they are, what they do, what it costs, what STAN's fee is. |
| Member | Show what membership includes, and let someone join. |
| Advocate | Give them something worth sharing — the belief register page. |

**The build rule:** the ladder is encoded as data in `data/services.yaml`, but
it is **not** rendered as a list on the front door. A price catalogue reads as
a shop, and a shop is not what earns this audience's trust. Each service
appears only where it is the natural next step, and the whole list lives on
one transparency page reached from the footer.

### 7.1 The funnel on the page

This table is the contract between this document and the website. Each row is
one stage, where it lives on the site, the single thing it asks for, and where
it hands the person on. If a section of the site asks for more than one thing,
or asks for nothing, it has drifted from this table.

| Stage | Where on the site | The one ask | Hands off to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Search bar, six doors | Pick a door. On an unmet search: what were you looking for? | The door's panel |
| First value | Route panel, marker and question pages, seminars, open groups | Nothing. Read, watch, sit in. | The route's **next step** card, chosen per door |
| First £ | The `next` card on three doors (using, stopped, bloods); the peer panel in Connect; the foot of every marker page | Book the walkthrough. Until booking is live: leave an email. | The consultation brief, then the clinical rung |
| Committed | The cohort in Groups, and the `next` card on the coming-off door | Join the waiting list | The cohort itself, then membership |
| Clinical | The clinical panel in Connect | "I would want this" — demand capture until a clinician exists | The clinician; STAN's fee printed |
| Member | Not on the site yet | — | — |
| Advocate | Not on the site yet | — | — |
| Transparency | Footer link: *What costs money here, and why* | Nothing. Read it. | Any service page |

Every capture on the site goes through one mechanism with one storage key,
tagged by stage, so that when a real mailing tool arrives it is wired in once.

### 7.2 Which door hands to what

| Door | Next step | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| Thinking about starting | Seminar: *What actually happens when you stop* | Know the exit before the entrance. Free, low commitment, right tone. |
| Using and staying on | Bloods walkthrough | The one thing that changes his odds is reading his own panel. |
| Want to come off | Coming-off cohort waiting list | Nobody should do the first six weeks alone. |
| Stopped and feel wrong | Bloods walkthrough | He has the sheet that said he was "normal". Start there. |
| Bloods I can't read | Bloods walkthrough | The obvious one. |
| Worried about someone | Partners and family group | He will get more from them than from the person he is worried about. |

Three of six doors hand to the walkthrough. That is the wedge doing its job.
The copy on each card is different because the person arriving is different.

## 8. Sequence

1. **This week.** Services encoded as data; each door hands to its next
   step; the walkthrough is in Connect; the transparency page exists. *Done.*
   The walkthrough goes first, **alone**. Peer sessions follow after the
   first ten, once the line between the two has been held in the room.
2. **Next.** Public URL, STAN email, a calendar link and a payment link. The
   walkthrough is then live — genuinely bookable, genuinely paid.
3. **First ten walkthroughs.** Learn what people actually bring, what they ask,
   where the line gets tested. Refine the brief.
4. **Month two.** Second facilitator recruited from among the first ten if one
   is suitable. Cohort waiting list opens.
5. **Month three.** First panel clinician — approached as "would you see people
   we send you", not "would you review a page".
6. **Month six.** Membership, once the library justifies it.
7. **PR** after all of the above, not before.
