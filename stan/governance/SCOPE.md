# STAN — Scope of Practice

**Status: DRAFT v0.1.** Not adopted. Requires legal review and sign-off by a
named clinical lead before any content is published against it.

---

## 1. Purpose

STAN (Steroid Awareness Network) exists to close a documented gap: men and women
who have used, are using, or are considering performance-enhancing drugs cannot
reliably get accurate, non-judgemental, evidence-anchored information about what
those drugs do to the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal (HPG) axis, or about how to
stop safely.

The gap is not anecdotal. A 2023 survey of endocrinologists managing recovery
from anabolic-androgenic steroid induced hypogonadism (ASIH) found no consistent
management approach and no agreed pathway (Grant et al., *Reproduction &
Fertility*, 2023). The clinical literature is thin, the guideline coverage is
partial, and the informational vacuum has been filled by commercially motivated
sources.

STAN's role is to map what is known, mark clearly where practice runs ahead of
evidence, and route people to clinicians who work in this area.

## 2. Who STAN is for

- People currently using PEDs who want to understand the physiological consequences.
- People who have stopped and are experiencing symptoms of ASIH.
- People considering use who want a realistic account of the risks.
- Partners and families.
- Clinicians who encounter these patients and have no pathway to follow.

STAN does not restrict access by sex or gender. Content on PED use in women is
in scope and is currently under-served everywhere; it is a priority, not an
afterthought.

## 3. What STAN does

1. **Publishes reviewed information.** Every information page is authored,
   clinically reviewed, cited to the evidence register, dated, and scheduled for
   re-review.
2. **Signposts to care.** Maintains routes into NHS services, harm-reduction
   services (including needle and syringe programmes), and vetted private
   clinicians.
3. **Produces consultation briefs.** Structured documents a user takes to their
   GP or specialist, containing their own results, the relevant guideline
   citation, and the specific question to ask. This converts "my GP doesn't
   know" into "my GP has something to work with."
4. **Publishes lived-experience accounts**, clearly labelled as such and
   governed separately from information content.
5. **Convenes** — seminars, recorded talks, and moderated connection between
   people going through the same thing.

## 4. What STAN does NOT do

This list is public, permanent, and not apologised for. It is the clearest
signal that STAN is different from the rest of this information ecosystem.

- **No individual medical advice.** STAN does not assess, diagnose, prescribe,
  or tell any specific person what to take, how much, or when.
- **No dosing protocols.** STAN does not publish cycles, stacks, dosages,
  titration schedules, or post-cycle therapy protocols.
- **No sourcing.** STAN does not tell anyone where to obtain controlled or
  prescription-only substances, and does not link to, review, or rank suppliers.
- **No supply.** STAN does not sell, supply, or broker any drug or supplement.
- **No affiliate income** from supplements, sources, coaches, laboratories, or
  clinics. See §7.
- **No claims of certainty where none exists.** Where the evidence is absent or
  contested, STAN says so plainly rather than filling the gap with confidence.
- **No content that assumes the reader will stop.** Harm reduction means meeting
  people where they are. It does not mean supplying a how-to.

## 5. The boundary rule

Every piece of STAN content must fall into exactly one of three classes, and be
visibly labelled as such:

| Class | Definition | Governance |
|---|---|---|
| **Information** | General statements about physiology, risk, tests, and treatment options that apply to a described population, not an individual. | Clinical review required. Every substantive claim cited to an approved source. |
| **Signposting** | Routes to a service, clinician, or organisation. | Verification of the service required. Re-checked at each review cycle. |
| **Lived experience** | First-person account of what happened to a named or pseudonymous individual. | Never framed as guidance. Carries a standing notice that one person's experience is not evidence. Reviewed for harm, not for clinical accuracy. |

If a draft cannot be placed cleanly in one class, it does not publish.

## 6. Red flags and escalation

Any content touching symptoms that may indicate an emergency — chest pain,
breathlessness, syncope, suicidal ideation, signs of thrombosis, severe
psychiatric disturbance — must terminate in an unambiguous instruction to seek
urgent care (999 / A&E / NHS 111 / Samaritans 116 123). This instruction appears
before any other information on the page, not in a footer.

STAN operates no crisis service and must never present itself as one.

## 7. Funding and conflicts of interest

- Information is free, permanently, without registration.
- STAN accepts no money from manufacturers or sellers of PEDs, supplements, or
  from any party whose income depends on people continuing to use.
- Where STAN refers to a paid partner service (consultation, phlebotomy,
  laboratory work), the commercial relationship and any fee to STAN is published
  on the page where the referral appears.
- Every contributor, reviewer, board member and partner completes a declaration
  of interests, held in a public register.
- **Blood testing:** STAN will not promote or refer to any testing service that
  does not include a route to clinical interpretation of the result. A panel
  without interpretation is a harm vector, not a service.

## 8. Standards STAN holds itself to

- **NHS content style guide** for readability, structure, and plain English.
  Adopted as a *writing* standard only; it carries no clinical authority here.
- **WCAG 2.2 AA** accessibility, treated as a launch requirement, not a backlog item.
- **PIF TICK** (Patient Information Forum) certification as a stated goal.
- **UK GDPR**, with all user data held in the UK or EEA. Any health-adjacent
  data about an identifiable person is special category data and is treated as such.

## 9. Safeguarding

Adolescents are a real and growing at-risk group and will reach this site. A
safeguarding policy covering under-18s and vulnerable adults must be adopted
before public launch. This document does not substitute for it.
