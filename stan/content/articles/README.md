# The article stack

Articles in waiting, and what each is still missing. An article moves out of
this file and into its own `.md` here only when the claims it needs exist,
their quotes are verified, and nothing in it asserts more than those claims do.

## How an article gets written

The order is fixed, and it is the opposite of the order that comes naturally:

1. **A question exists.** From `knowledge/questions/` — 68 catalogued, drawn
   from what people actually ask. Not from what would be interesting to write.
2. **Claims exist that bear on it.** From `knowledge/claims/`, each with its
   verbatim quote and its page.
3. **The quotes are verified.** Somebody has read the quote against the source.
4. **The article is composed from the claims.** Nothing in the prose may assert
   what no claim carries. Where the claims run out, the article says so.
5. **Second reader, then clinical review** where the interim standard requires
   it (`governance/INTERIM-STANDARD.md`).

Step 4 is the one under pressure. The temptation is always to write the good
sentence and then find a citation for it. That is how the first attempt at the
encyclopedia produced fifteen records citing nothing.

**How it is written** is `governance/EDITORIAL-VOICE.md`. The spine of it: true,
proven and safe are three different questions, and the job is to find the
specific untested link rather than to fight everything.

**Two rules that override everything here.** No dosing, cycle length, stacking
or tapering figures reach a published page, whatever the claims record
(`governance/SCOPE.md`). And where a source reports a belief users hold, the
article says so in the same breath — never repeats the belief in STAN's own
voice.

## Stack

### 0. The sentence that gets left out — DRAFTED

**File:** `the-sentence-that-gets-left-out.md`
**Answers:** no catalogued question directly. It teaches a habit rather than
answering a question, which makes it the odd one out here.
**Claims used:** `stanozolol-non-aromatizable-by-structure`,
`no-evidence-alternating-steroids-potentiates`,
`stacking-evidence-comes-from-livestock`,
`supratherapeutic-doses-increase-mass-and-strength`
**Blocked by:** quote verification; a current evidence check on alternating and
stacking; clinical review (tier B).

Written first because it demonstrates the voice rather than because it is the
most needed. It takes one paragraph of Taylor — stanozolol does not aromatise,
alternating might therefore potentiate, no direct evidence supports it — and
uses it to teach the split between true, proven and safe. If the tone is wrong,
it is cheaper to find out here than on an article somebody urgently needs.

### 1. The break between cycles — what the evidence actually says

**Answers:** `do-i-need-pct`, `taper-or-stop`
**Claims available:** `no-evidence-for-rest-phase-between-cycles`
**Still needed:** a current evidence check. The claim is a 2002 statement that
evidence was thin. Twenty-four years is long enough for that to have changed,
and publishing "there's no evidence" when there now is would be worse than
saying nothing.

The strongest candidate in the stack. A practice that is close to universal in
the community, and a physician in print saying the evidence for it was thin.
That is a genuine, useful, non-obvious thing to tell somebody — and it is
exactly the kind of thing no forum will tell them. **Write this one first,
after the evidence check.**

### 2. Why the same dose stops working

**Answers:** `is-it-too-late-for-me`, and part of `am-i-addicted`
**Claims available:** `aas-increases-myonuclei-and-androgen-receptors`,
`dose-escalation-mechanism-is-theory`, `enzyme-induction-escalation-unproven`
**Still needed:** refs 13, 14, 15 from the chase list, plus current work on
androgen receptor density.

Escalation is what turns one cycle into ten years. Somebody who understands the
proposed mechanism is better placed than somebody who just experiences it. The
line to hold: explaining why escalation happens is not endorsing it, and the
piece must not read as permission. The author's own hedge — "this theory needs
further investigation" — belongs in the article, not just the record.

### 3. The dip after you stop, and why nobody warned you

**Answers:** `still-feel-awful-months-later`, `depression-from-stopping`
**Claims available:** `first-withdrawal-loss-and-low-mood`,
`first-cycle-users-report-psychological-gains`
**Still needed:** a current source on mood after cessation. The 2002 passage is
descriptive and unquantified, which is enough to say "this is a known and named
experience" and not enough for anything more.

Aimed squarely at the largest group arriving at STAN. Its value is recognition
rather than information: the experience is described in a published source, by
a physician, as an ordinary part of the pattern. To somebody who has been told
their bloods are fine, being told the experience is real and named is worth
more than a mechanism.

**Handle with care.** `first-cycle-users-report-psychological-gains` contains
the sentence "they conclude that anabolic steroids are harmless supplements",
which is the author describing a mistaken belief. Quoted loose, it inverts.

### 4. "Muscle memory" — what is actually established

**Answers:** not yet catalogued. Add the question first.
**Claims available:** `aas-increases-myonuclei-and-androgen-receptors`
**Still needed:** most of it. Refs 13–14, whether they are human or rodent, and
a proper search of the modern myonuclear literature, which is substantial and
genuinely contested.

High traffic, high risk, and the piece most likely to be searched for. The
community claim is that myonuclei are permanent and confer a lasting advantage.
The 2002 sentence does not say that. It would be easy, and wrong, to let this
article imply it does. **Not writeable on current holdings** — listed here so
the gap between what STAN has and what this needs stays visible.

### 5. "Your bloods came back normal"

**Answers:** `bloods-normal-but-feel-terrible`, `how-do-i-know-axis-restarted`
**Claims available:** `gains-largely-retained-three-months-after-stopping`
**Still needed:** ref 12, and Grant 2023 read in full.

The interesting thing here is an absence. The passage lists what returns to
normal after stopping — blood pressure, lipoproteins, liver enzymes — and
testosterone, LH and FSH are not on the list. An article can be built on
noticing that, but only once ref 12 is read and STAN knows whether the omission
is the study's or the author's summary. **Do not write this from the book
alone.**

## What the stack is short of

All five need work outside the book, which is the honest position: one
twenty-four-year-old secondary source cannot carry an article by itself, and
should not.

Two things unblock most of it, in order:

1. **Verify the ten quotes** against pages 60–62. The book is in hand, the
   quotes are transcribed from photographs, and none has been checked
   character by character. An hour, and it moves ten claims from draft to
   checked.
2. **Photograph the Chapter 5 endnotes.** See
   `evidence/reference-chase/taylor-2002-chapter-5.md`. One photo turns this
   into twenty-six primary leads.

Both are the founder's, and neither needs a clinician.
