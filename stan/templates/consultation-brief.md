# Consultation brief

**What this is:** a structure for organising your own information before an
appointment, so that a clinician who does not specialise in this area has
something concrete to work with.

**What this is not:** a diagnosis, a treatment plan, or a script to persuade a
clinician of a conclusion you have already reached. It contains questions, not
answers. If you fill it in and the answer to a question turns out to be "your
reasoning is wrong", that is the brief working correctly.

Print it, fill it in, and hand it over at the start of the appointment.

## 5. Questions I would like to ask

Delete what does not apply. Add your own.

1. My total testosterone is reported as within range. Given my SHBG result, would
   a **calculated free testosterone** be informative here? UK Society for
   Endocrinology guidance notes that where SHBG is abnormal, total testosterone
   may not reflect biological androgen effect. *(cite:
   jayasena-2022-sfe-trt-guidelines — pending approval)*
2. Is my current route of administration and dose likely to be achieving adequate
   levels for me, and how would we know?
3. What monitoring schedule should I be on, and which tests?
4. Is referral to endocrinology, andrology, or a male fertility service
   appropriate in my case?
5. If fertility matters to me, what should be assessed, and when?
6. What would you expect to change, and over what timescale, if we adjusted
   anything?
7. What would make you concerned enough to want to see me sooner?

**A note on how to use this.** Handing a clinician a document can land badly if
it reads as a challenge. It lands well when it reads as preparation. If you only
take one thing, take Section 4 with the reference ranges filled in — organised
results are useful to any clinician, whatever they conclude.
